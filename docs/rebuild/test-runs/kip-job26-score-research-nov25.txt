--- A1. TEAM PREVIEW / BRING-4 & LEAD CHOICE (EXPECTED-VALUE-OVER-BRINGS VS WHOLE-SIX) ---
- What the evidence says:
  - `VGC-Bench` (arXiv:2506.10326v1, Jun 12, 2025, T1: https://arxiv.org/html/2506.10326v1) formalizes VGC team preview as a
    combinatorial subteam selection problem with `(6 choose 2) * (4 choose 2) = 90` ordered lead/back configurations per player
    (15 unordered Bring-4 subsets). Generalizing across diverse 6-Pokémon toolboxes is identified as a primary bottleneck.
  - Carli (Journal of Geek Studies, Jul 11, 2025, T1/T3: https://jgeekstudies.org/2025/07/11/predicting-competitive-pokemon-vgc-leads-using-latent-semantic-analysis-a-data-driven-approach-to-team-matchups/)
    trained Latent Semantic Analysis on >5,000 Showdown logs (1,174 filtered) and evaluated on all 32 NAIC 2025 Masters Top 8 games:
    top-3 predicted leads achieved 62.50% Hard Prediction (both leads exact) and 81.25% Soft Prediction (at least 1 lead; 83.33% /
    91.66% in Finals) — proving the opponent's 6 strongly constrains Bring-4 choice.
  - Open-source VGC agents (`EliteFurretAI`, T2: https://github.com/caymansimpson/EliteFurretAI; `pokechamp`, Aug 16, 2025, T2:
    https://github.com/sethkarten/pokechamp/releases) separate Team Preview selection from in-battle play.
  - NastyPlot's own `docs/strength-score.md` (Nov 9, 2026, Internal): on 13,265 Showdown ladder replays (`four-synergy.mjs`),
    "Two lead Pokémon together: nothing beyond which four were brought" and "Synergy inside the brought four: unknown (too little data)".
- Can expected-value-over-brings `E[x(B4_A) - x(B4_B)]` beat whole-six modeling?
  - Theoretically YES for head-to-head matchup prediction (a niche 6th tech mon like Gastrodon or Amoonguss shouldn't drag down a
    six when it sits on the bench 60% of games). Practically, for our Limitless tournament `out/matches.json`, we do NOT observe
    which 4 were brought — only `out/replays.json` (Showdown ladder, ~13k games) has brought/lead data, with distribution shift
    between ladder Bo1 and Limitless open-sheet tournaments.
- Expected gain range: `-0.0002` to `-0.0008` LL if bring-rate-weighted species weights `p_bring(mon | team)` are transferred from
  ladder replays; up to `-0.0015` LL only if tournament replay bring-4 logs become available.
- Overfitting risk: HIGH if fitting a full 15x15 subteam matchup matrix; MEDIUM if restricted to marginal bring probability `p(bring_i | team)`.
- Data we have vs lack: Have ladder replays (`out/replays.json`, `leads.mjs`); LACK brought-4 / lead labels on Limitless tournament rows.

--- A2. SET-LEVEL FEATURES BEYOND TODAY'S CENTERED SETS (HIERARCHICAL SHRINKAGE BY SPECIES) ---
- What the evidence says:
  - Today's `model.mjs` uses flat `lc = 60` across all `Mon@Item`, `Mon#Ability`, `Mon:Move` features passing `>= 40` decks, with
    unweighted season-long centering `freq[j]`.
  - Live Champions Reg M-C usage (MunchStats, Nov 2026, T3: https://www.munchstats.com/) shows extreme skew: staple moves like
    Sneasler Close Combat appear on 99.9% of sets (near-zero variance), while tech items/moves vary sharply between staples
    (2,000+ decks) and niche species (50 decks).
  - High-dimensional logistic regression theory (Bai et al., ICML 2021, T1: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/bai21c/bai21c.pdf)
    shows flat L2 regularization under-shrinks low-count groups while over-shrinking high-count groups, inflating logit variance.
  - Hierarchical empirical-Bayes shrinkage scales each set feature's penalty inversely with species sample size (`lc_j ~ lc * sqrt(medSp / spDecks[sp])`),
    and recency-weighted centering (`freq_t[j]` with 14d half-life) stops mid-season set shifts (e.g., an item moving from 20% to 70%
    usage) from leaking into species intercept `b[sp]`.
  - Stat points / Tera: Champions has NO Terastallization (Tera absent by rule), and Limitless open team sheets do NOT publish
    EV/stat-point spreads (only Showdown ladder usage dumps have spreads).
- Expected gain range: `-0.0003` to `-0.0009` LL on top of `combo` (largest mid-to-late regulation when meta builds evolve).
- Overfitting risk: LOW (adds 0 new free parameters; only refines centering weights and per-species shrinkage scaling).
- Data we have vs lack: Have items, abilities, and moves per deck in `M.feats`/`M.decks`; LACK stat-point spreads on Limitless sheets.

--- A3. SPEED-CONTROL & ARCHETYPE INTERACTIONS (LOW-RANK VS EXPLICIT FEATURES) ---
- What the evidence says:
  - Chen & Joachims Blade-Chest (ACM WSDM 2016, T1: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/~shuochen/pubs/wsdm16_chen.pdf) proves low-rank
    antisymmetric bilinear forms `u(A).v(B) - u(B).v(A)` capture arbitrary rock-paper-scissors cycles. Our Run 2 already confirmed
    Blade-Chest `d=4` delivers `-0.00075` LL standalone and `-0.00125` LL inside `combo`.
  - However, Blade-Chest in `v3-lab.mjs` operates on SPECIES (`tf[A].f`), NOT deck-level moves/abilities! A Farigiraf or Tornadus
    carrying Trick Room vs Imprison, or a team with Tailwind + Sun vs Hard Trick Room, is a DECK-level archetype property.
  - NastyPlot's `docs/strength-score.md` (Nov 9, 2026) showed marginal main effects for speed control (`-0.6 pts`) were flat because
    ~92% of teams carry *some* speed control — the real signal is the **interaction** (Trick Room vs Tailwind vs Rain vs Sun vs Snow).
  - An explicit 6-dim deck archetype vector `[TR, TW, Sun, Rain, Sand, Snow]` with an antisymmetric 6x6 matchup matrix `W` has ONLY
    `6 * 5 / 2 = 15` free parameters (vs `~300 * 8 = 2,400` in species Blade-Chest), making it ultra-low-variance and interpretable.
- Expected gain range: `-0.0003` to `-0.0010` LL on top of `combo` (captures move-level TR/TW/weather matchups invisible to species-only `U, V`).
- Overfitting risk: VERY LOW (only 15 antisymmetric parameters with L2=20).
- Data we have vs lack: Have exact moves and abilities in `M.feats`/`M.decks` (`:Trick Room`, `:Tailwind`, `#Drought`, `#Drizzle`, `#Sand Stream`, `#Snow Warning`, `#Mega Sol`).

--- A4. TIME: RECENCY HALF-LIFE PER COMPONENT & META DRIFT DETECTION ---
- What the evidence says:
  - Dynamic Bradley-Terry literature (Ley et al., arXiv:1807.01623v2, Aug 3, 2018, T1: https://arxiv.org/html/1807.01623v2;
    Bong et al., AISTATS 2020, T1: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v108/bong20a/bong20a.pdf; Coulom WHR, 2008, T1:
    https://www.remi-coulom.fr/WHR/WHR.pdf) finds time-varying models consistently beat static BT, AND different latent factors
    evolve on different timescales.
  - In `model.mjs` / `combo`, player skill `s[p]` currently shares the 14-day half-life (`half = 14`) used for Pokémon meta shifts.
    When a strong player takes 3 weeks off, their prior games drop to `0.5^(21/14) = 0.35x` weight — leaking their skill into their
    Pokémon! Decoupling `skillHalf = 60` days (slow skill drift) from `half = 14` days (fast meta drift) fixes this cleanly without
    the online-update instability that hurt Glicko-2 in Run 1 (`+0.00139` LL).
  - Meta drift detection: ADWIN (Bifet & Gavaldà, SDM 2007, T1: https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220907178_Learning_from_Time-Changing_Data_with_Adaptive_Windowing;
    River docs, T2: https://riverml.xyz/dev/api/drift/ADWIN/; window-dilemma analysis arXiv:2602.06456v1, Feb 6, 2026, T1:
    https://arxiv.org/html/2602.06456v1) monitors rolling prediction log-loss or top-20 usage KL-divergence to flag sudden meta
    shifts (e.g., post-Regional breakout team or balance patch) and shorten `half` dynamically.
- Expected gain range: `-0.0003` to `-0.0010` LL for decoupled `skillHalf=60` on top of `combo`; drift detection is mainly a monitoring/stability win.
- Overfitting risk: VERY LOW (1 hyperparameter `skillHalf`, zero added model parameters).
- Data we have vs lack: Have exact tournament dates `evDates` and persistent Limitless account IDs `pa, pb`.

--- A5. CALIBRATION BY SEGMENT & COMMUNICATING UNCERTAINTY HONESTLY ---
- What the evidence says:
  - Guo et al. (ICML 2017, T1: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v70/guo17a.html) established single-parameter temperature scaling as
    the gold-standard baseline, while Bai et al. (ICML 2021, T1: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v139/bai21c/bai21c.pdf) showed
    high-dimensional logistic models are systematically overconfident on rarer feature combinations.
  - Semantic-Aware Group Calibration GC+TS (arXiv:2306.04985v2, Oct 21, 2023, T1: https://arxiv.org/html/2306.04985v2) proves
    Theorem 1: group-wise temperature scaling is strictly **accuracy-preserving** while reducing Expected Calibration Error (ECE)
    and log-loss across subgroups with different signal-to-noise ratios.
  - Look at our Run 2 robustness splits: `combo` had `acc = 55.50%+0.42` (`ll = 0.6804`) on `<64` events vs `53.79%+0.54` (`ll = 0.6854`)
    on `64+` events, and `+0.99 pts` (`-0.00219` LL) on familiar teams (`bothCommon`) vs `+0.42 pts` on rarer teams, with overall
    `calSlope = 0.904` (slightly overconfident!). Regularized 4-segment temperature scaling `(common/rare x small/big)` shrunk toward
    global `w0` lets `innerCal` temper overconfident rare-team matchups while keeping sharp odds on familiar matchups.
  - Communicating uncertainty: van der Bles et al. (PNAS, Mar 2020, T1: https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1913678117) proved
    showing a numeric range alongside a point estimate ("82, likely 78–86") communicates uncertainty honestly WITHOUT lowering user trust.
- Expected gain range: `-0.0002` to `-0.0007` LL on top of `combo` (pure calibration improvement, zero accuracy regression by Theorem 1).
- Overfitting risk: VERY LOW (4 slopes strongly L2-shrunk toward `w0` with `lamSeg = 15`, fitted strictly on `innerCal`).
- Data we have vs lack: Have exact `evPlayers` and train-only `trTeamCnt` inside every walk-forward cut.

================================================================================
PART D — FALSIFICATION CHECKS OF COMBO ITSELF
================================================================================
Implemented in `experiments/v3-lab.mjs` (run via `--falsify-all`, or individually):
1. `--falsify-shuffle` (Within-Event Team-Label Permutation):
   - Permutes `(team, deck)` assignments across games within each event while preserving event dates, player IDs, and outcomes,
     then runs full walk-forward `baseline` vs `combo`.
   - S7 falsification criterion: `combo`'s gain MUST vanish (`95% CI` includes `0` or `|dLL| <= 0.0003`). If `combo` still beats
     `baseline` with `dLL < -0.0003` and CI `< 0`, there is hidden leakage or a player-skill artifact.
2. `--falsify-reverse` (Time-Reversed Split — DIAGNOSTIC ONLY, NOT PRIMARY EVIDENCE):
   - Trains strictly on LATER events `> cut` and tests strictly on EARLIER events `(cut - 7d, cut]`.
   - S7 diagnostic interpretation: Helps distinguish genuine structural matchup/shrinkage signal (which should remain positive in
     reverse) from a lucky late-season regime fluke. Never used as primary ship evidence (Rule 21 requires strictly future games).
3. `--falsify-loeo` (Leave-One-Event-Out Influence):
   - Computes exact per-event `deltaLL` contribution across all pooled test events, prints Top-5 most favorable and Top-5 least
     favorable events, and checks `maxSingleEventShare = max_e(-contrib_e) / (-totalDeltaLL)`.
   - S7 falsification criterion: Flags `WARN` if any single tournament drives `> 25%` of `combo`'s total log-loss gain, or if
     dropping the single most favorable event flips `loeo_dLL >= 0`.
