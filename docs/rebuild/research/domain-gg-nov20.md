# Is .gg worth it for NastyPlot? (Nov 20, 2026)

Look-only research for Sim. Nothing bought, registered or signed up for. Nobody contacted. No personal data shared.
Checks run Fri Nov 20, 2026, from 8:06 PM EST (clock: `TZ=America/New_York date` gave `Fri Nov 20 20:06:16 EST 2026` at the start and `20:10:09` when the numbers were totalled).
Labels: **checked** (source or command + result) or **my judgement / not checked**. Prices are USD and can change any day. Not legal advice.

## Summary (8 lines)

1. **Verdict: yes to nastyplot.gg, but with conditions** (my judgement). It costs about $37 a year more than .app. The gaming meaning is real. But I found one rule the earlier report missed (line 4).
2. **Cost (Porkbun, checked live today):** .gg $51.80 every year, with no first-year discount. .app $8.75, then $14.93. **Gap: $43 in year 1, $36.87 a year after. Over 5 years: .gg $259.00 vs .app $68.47 ($190.53 more). Over 10 years: $518.00 vs $143.12 ($374.88 more).**
3. **Price risk is moderate, not high.** I found no rise in the registry's own .gg price since at least 2021. The 2021 "price rise" story was one registrar's retail change; the registry said it had not raised its price (checked). But the registry can change prices with no outside cap. .ai went up 14% in March 2026 (checked).
4. **Risk 1, privacy (new finding).** The .gg rules say a website on a .gg name "used for a commercial purpose" must show its owner's identity on the site: name, postal and email address, and more (clause 4.4, checked). A free site with no ads probably isn't "commercial" (my judgement). Ads, donations or affiliate links could change that. .gg also allows no privacy service that hides the owner from the registry (clause 8.9, checked).
5. **Risk 2, losing the name by accident.** A .gg that isn't renewed is gone from the registry about 35 days after it expires (checked: AWS Route 53 rules). Auto-renew with a backup card is a must.
6. **Risk 3, search.** Google's own list of country endings it treats as "global" leaves out .gg (checked again today; .io, .ai, .tv, .co, .me are on it). So Google may link .gg sites to Guernsey. op.gg and u.gg rank worldwide anyway, so the real effect looks small (my judgement). Sources disagree.
7. **No sign of .gg being a problem** for school filters (they block by category, such as "games", not by ending), for email, or for abuse. The .gg phishing rate is about 0.01% of names (ICANN data for May 2023, quoted by the registry's own director). DNSSEC works (checked: signed, validates). Guernsey has no .io-style sovereignty question (my judgement).
8. **Cheapest safe route:** buy nastyplot.gg at Porkbun ($51.80/yr) or Spaceship (~$51.60/yr), or prepay at Dynadot (10 years $481.50, which works out at $48.15/yr). Turn on auto-renew and the registrar lock. Also buy nastyplot.app ($8.75, then $14.93) as the fallback and redirect it. Keep the site free of ads until Sim has decided on clause 4.4, or start on .app and send .gg to it.

## 2. Who runs .gg, and what the rules say

### 2.1 The registry

- **Manager:** Island Networks Ltd (checked: [IANA delegation record](https://www.iana.org/domains/root/db/gg.html), last updated 2025-07-07). The registration agreement names the contracting registry as "CI Domain Registry Ltd" (checked). The public site is [channelisles.net](https://www.channelisles.net/), which runs .gg and .je, "1996-2026: 30 years" (checked).
- Founded in 1996 by Nigel Roberts and Laurie Brown (checked: channelisles.net). One registrar's site says management passed to the States of Guernsey in 2000 (my note: [domainnames.gg history page](https://www.domainnames.gg/domain-names/a-brief-history-of-gg-domain-names/); I'm unsure who runs that site, and IANA still lists Island Networks, so treat this as unconfirmed).
- **Open to anyone since 2019.** No need to live in Guernsey (checked: agreement clause 17.6: "no restrictions whatsoever as to who may register").
- **Security:** joined the European TLD-ISAC security group in Nov 2024 (checked: [Bailiwick Express](https://www.bailiwickexpress.com/gsy/news/gg-owner-joins-european-cybersecurity-body)). It offers a "GG Shield" anti-hijack service (checked: domainnames.gg page; not confirmed on the registry's own site).
- **Name servers:** the .gg zone is served by Nominet's servers (dns1-4.nominetdns.uk) plus the registry's own (c.ci-servers.org, e.ci-servers.net). checked: `cloudflare-dns.com/dns-query?name=gg&type=NS`. Nominet runs .uk, so the DNS has a large, experienced operator behind it.
- **DNSSEC: yes.** checked: the DS record for `gg` exists and the answer came back validated (`"AD":true`). Route 53 also says DNSSEC is supported for .gg names ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/Route53/latest/DeveloperGuide/gg.html)).
- **Outages, seizures:** I found none (checked: searches; nothing found is not proof there were none).

### 2.2 Rules that matter for Sim (Registration Agreement v25.01)

All checked: [channelisles.net/legal/terms-and-conditions-1](https://www.channelisles.net/legal/terms-and-conditions-1/).

- **4.4 Owner identity on commercial sites.** If a .gg website "is being used for a commercial purpose", it must show "in a reasonably prominent place" the identifying information English law asks of commercial websites. At minimum: company name, postal and email address, registration number, place of incorporation, a non-electronic contact, trade bodies.
  - Why it matters: Sim wants privacy, and the site is for kids too. My judgement: a free site with no ads, donations or affiliate links is probably not "commercial". Ads, a tip jar or affiliate links could make it so. Before any money comes in, Sim would need a business address, such as a company or a mail-forwarding address. **Unsure; not legal advice.**
  - .app and .net have no such rule (my judgement, from ICANN gTLD norms; not checked line by line).
- **8.9 No privacy services that hide the owner from the registry.** 14.5: the registry does "not publish any personal information contained in the 'name' or 'address' fields", and 14.6 says it won't publish email addresses. So the public WHOIS should not show Sim's name, but the registry holds it.
  - Route 53 lists .gg "Privacy protection: Not supported" (checked). Porkbun and Dynadot show privacy as unsupported (checked: tld-list, Dynadot).
  - I could not run a live WHOIS to see what is actually shown (port 43 to whois.gg gave no output from this machine).
- **14.10 Disclosure.** The registry may give registration data to anyone with a "Legitimate Interest", naming "persons acting on behalf of intellectual property rightsholders". For a Pokémon fan site, that would include The Pokémon Company's lawyers.
  - My judgement: .app/.net privacy services also hand over data on a proper legal request. So this is a small difference, not a big one.
- **3.5 Reputation clause.** The registry may cancel a name used in a way "which could damage the reputation of the Channel Islands", "on giving you reasonable notice and an opportunity for independent review and appeal". My judgement: no real risk for a Pokémon team builder.
- **4.2-4.3 Accurate details.** The registry may suspend or cancel without notice for false registration details. It may cancel if an inaccuracy isn't fixed within 7 days of notice. A valid email is required (11.4, 14.8).
  - A search summary mentioned a blog story of .gg names revoked over a US PO box address. I could not find that story on the page it pointed to (not checked; treat as rumour). The lesson still stands: use a real, current address and email.
- **13 Spam.** One upheld spam complaint can mean a temporary suspension; repeats can mean the name is taken away.
- **15.2 Law of the British Islands.** The name can be suspended for breaking UK or Channel Islands law. (US sites on .com live under US law instead.)
- **17.1 Governing law:** English law.
- **Disputes:** handled by the registry's own process, CI-DRS (mediation, then a formal claim), not ICANN's UDRP. checked: [channelisles.net/legal/dispute-resolution](https://www.channelisles.net/legal/dispute-resolution) is listed, and clause 12 confirms a court can be asked before any transfer.
- **Term:** 1 year at minimum (2.4). Route 53 sells 1-year terms only. Dynadot sells up to 10.

### 2.3 Transfers, expiry, RDAP

- **Transfers** need an auth code, and a registrar lock is supported (checked: AWS Route 53 .gg page).
- **Expiry is unforgiving** (checked: AWS): late renewal up to 29 days after expiry, restore with the registry between 30 and 35 days, **deleted from the registry at 35 days**. After that, anyone can register it (clause 3.3, checked). A .com gets about 75 days in total.
- **RDAP:** IANA's RDAP list has no entry for .gg (checked: `data.iana.org/rdap/dns.json` returns nothing for gg; `rdap.org/domain/pkmn.gg` gives 404). WHOIS is at whois.gg. The registry charges £3 per lookup outside its licence terms (checked: [WHOIS terms](https://www.channelisles.net/legal/whoisterms/), search snippet).

### 2.4 Country risk compared

- **.io:** the UK and Mauritius signed the Chagos treaty on May 22, 2025. It is not yet in force: the UK bill fell when the 2025-26 session of Parliament ended on April 29, 2026, and talks go on (checked: [NameSilo blog](https://www.namesilo.com/blog/en/domain-names/what-happens-to-io-domains-the-chagos-agreement-and-your-options), [Commons Library](https://commonslibrary.parliament.uk/research-briefings/cbp-10273/)). ICANN says even a change of sovereignty would not end .io at once. If IO were removed from the ISO list, a phase-out of about 5 years would follow (checked: [ICANN blog, Nov 2024](https://www.icann.org/en/blogs/details/the-chagos-archipelago-and-the-io-domain-14-11-2024-en)). .io still works today.
- **.gg:** Guernsey is a stable Crown dependency, and its code is not under any dispute I know of (my judgement, not checked). I found no sovereignty question.
- **The real country-ending risks for .gg** (my judgement): price is set by one small registry with no outside cap; local law (UK/Channel Islands) applies; the rules above can change "from time to time".

## 3. Is .gg worth it for this site?

### 3.1 Do gamers and VGC players recognise .gg?

- These all resolve today (checked: DNS A records via Cloudflare DoH): **rk9.gg** (the official Pokémon tournament sign-up and pairings tool that VGC players use), **pkmn.gg**, op.gg, u.gg, start.gg, tracker.gg, mobalytics.gg.
- But the big Pokémon reference sites are mostly .com or .net: pikalytics.com, limitlessvgc.com, battlefy.com, pokemonshowdown.com, serebii.net (checked: DNS now and in the earlier report). pokepast.es is a word-play ending.
- Wikipedia notes many games, streamers and esports sites use .gg for "good game" (checked: [Wikipedia .gg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/.gg)).
- **I found no data** on click-through, trust or type-in traffic by ending for .gg (not checked: nothing found). The one trust survey I know of (GrowthBadger, earlier report) didn't include .gg.
- My judgement: VGC players already type rk9.gg, so .gg looks normal and "for competitive players" to them. It adds flavour, not trust that .app lacks. And for both endings, people who misremember will guess nastyplot.com, which a stranger owns.

### 3.2 Parents, schools, filters

- I found no evidence that schools or parental filters block .gg as an ending. District pages say filters (GoGuardian, Lightspeed, Securly, iBoss) block categories such as "gaming and gambling" (checked: search results incl. [Yazoo City schools help page](https://yazoocityk12ms.freshdesk.com/support/solutions/articles/156000371599-why-are-certain-websites-or-apps-blocked-), [BVSD filtering categories](https://www.bvsd.org/departments/information-technology/information-security/internet-safety-web-filtering-at-bvsd/filtering-categories)).
- My judgement: a .gg ending may make a filter more likely to file NastyPlot under "games", but the content will do that anyway. The word "nasty" is the bigger filter risk (earlier report). Parents who don't game may not know .gg; .app reads more neutral.

### 3.3 Email

- A 2023 test of SpamAssassin found "no significant difference" in delivery by ending; Spamhaus says filters judge the sender's reputation, not the ending (checked via search summary: [ICANN UA list PDF](https://lists.iana.org/hyperkitty/list/ua-discuss@icann.org/message/TGBA4P6G4OSH74XRLL5GB43CBT5TXTBD/attachment/4/EmailDeliverabilityTesting2023.pdf), [Spamhaus](https://www.spamhaus.com/resource-center/xyz-on-new-domains/)). Neither tested .gg directly.
- My judgement: no email problem expected. NastyPlot barely sends email anyway (no accounts, COPPA).

### 3.4 Google search

- **Google's own page:** it treats some country endings as global and lists them. **.gg is not on the list**; .ai, .io, .tv, .co, .me are (checked again today: `curl` of [Google's multi-regional page](https://developers.google.com/search/docs/specialty/international/managing-multi-regional-sites), grep shows .ai .co .io .me .tv, no .gg).
- **Sources disagree:** [tld-list (updated Oct 19, 2026)](https://tld-list.com/blog/the-gg-domain-name) says Google treats .gg as Guernsey only. [Nominus](https://www.nominus.com/blog/gg-domain-gaming-island-code) (a .gg seller) says .gg is treated as global. Google's page supports tld-list.
- My judgement, not checked: Google dropped its "target a country" setting in Search Console in 2022, so there is no switch to flip. In practice, op.gg, u.gg and mobalytics.gg rank well worldwide, and Google uses many other signals (language, links, who visits). For a new site that depends on search, this is a real but small unknown. .app has no such question (Google treats new gTLDs like .com, Search Central 2015, earlier report).

### 3.5 Abuse reputation

- .gg: on average 0.01% of names flagged as phishing, 0.00% malware, 0.01% spam (ICANN DAAR figures for May 2023, quoted by the registry's director; checked: Bailiwick Express article). The source is the registry itself, so treat it with care.
- .gg is not in the Interisle/Cybercrime Information Center top-20 phishing tables for May-Jul 2026; those tables show .com 17.2 and .net 15.3 phishing names per 10,000 (checked: [Cybercrime Info Center](https://www.cybercrimeinfocenter.org/phishing-activity-in-tlds-may-july-2026)). Not being in a top-20 list doesn't prove a low rate.
- Spamhaus's live TLD table: not checked (Firecrawl credits ran out; it failed to load in the earlier report).
- My judgement: .gg's reputation is clean. Its high price keeps throwaway spam names away.

## 5. Recommendation for Sim

**Is .gg worth the extra $37 a year (about $190 over 5 years)? Yes, if Sim likes it, with three conditions** (my judgement):

1. **Keep NastyPlot non-commercial on .gg, or plan for clause 4.4.** No ads, donations or affiliate links on the .gg address unless Sim is ready to show a business name and postal address on the site. Reason: the .gg rules require it for commercial sites, and Sim's privacy matters more than the ending.
2. **Never let it lapse.** Auto-renew on, a backup card, and the registrar lock on. Or prepay 5-10 years at Dynadot. Reason: .gg names are deleted 35 days after expiry, and someone could grab it.
3. **Also buy nastyplot.app** ($8.75 now, $14.93 a year) and point it at .gg. Reason: a cheap fallback if .gg prices jump or the rules ever bite. Switching then is one redirect.

**Where I disagree with the earlier report:**
- It was right that .gg fits a "get better at the game" tool. But it didn't mention clause 4.4 (identity on commercial sites), the no-privacy-service rule, or the 35-day deletion. Those make .gg more of a commitment than the price alone suggests.
- It overstated the 2021 "registry price rise"; the registry denied raising its price.
- On value, the evidence for .gg is only that well-known gaming sites use it. There is no hard data that it brings more visitors. If Sim wants the cheapest clean choice, **.app alone is the honest budget pick, and it loses little that can be measured.**

**When:** buy both in December 2026, well before the January launch (my judgement). Reason: older names are less often blocked by new-domain filters, and holding them early costs only the yearly fee. Nobody else seems to want nastyplot.gg (no DNS, not registered, checked), but there is no point waiting.

**Cheapest safe way:** Porkbun for both (.gg $51.80 with a $1 first-year coupon, .app $8.75): **about $60 in year 1, $66.73 a year after.** Or prepay .gg for 10 years at Dynadot ($481.50 = $48.15/yr), which also locks the price. Avoid Namecheap ($82.98 renewal) and Gandi ($199.98 renewal) for .gg.
